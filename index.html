The brief and why the expo matters

What the tender asks for, and what changed in August

The tender gives the expo one line. Under Venue Overlays, clause (m) says: “Sports conclave/expo shall be part of the BRICS Games 2026 and overlays requirement provision for the same to be catered.” It says nothing about size, format, who exhibits, who pays or who sells stalls. This document fills that gap.

Why a worked-out expo plan scores

The 40-mark presentation rewards approach, execution and innovation. A clear expo plan with named exhibitors, a floor model and a lead-to-deal flow earns marks in all three, because no other bidder will have more than the one line from clause (m).

What changed in August 2026

  • The BRICS Sports Ministers' joint declaration (Visakhapatnam, 23 Aug 2026) describes the Ahmedabad Games as “a non-competitive and culturally focused initiative”. Ten countries attended: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Egypt, Ethiopia, Indonesia, UAE and Iran. (ANI)
  • The same meeting welcomed India's proposal for a BRICS Sports Manufacturing Conclave in Udaipur, November 2026, for manufacturers, exporters, investors, SMEs, start-ups and research bodies. (IndiasNews)
  • It also set up a Working Group on Sports Technology and committed to para-sport and women's participation. Dr Mandaviya's pitch: “Make in India, Play in India and Grow with India.” (Asianet Newsable)
  • Ahmedabad was confirmed as host of the 2030 Commonwealth Games on 26 Nov 2025, with 15 to 17 sports. Surfaces, equipment and venue-tech firms now have tenders to chase in this city. (SportsPro)
  • India holds the BRICS chair in 2026. (BRICS India 2026 official site)
Showcase in Ahmedabad. Deals in Udaipur.

The Games have one competition day. A trade fair needs buyers and time. A one-day heritage event has crowds, ministers and cameras. So the expo does three jobs: it puts BRICS traditional sports and their makers in front of delegations and media; it shows India's sports supply base to buyers and to the 2030 Commonwealth Games pipeline; and it captures qualified leads and hands them to the Udaipur conclave as pre-booked meetings.

Terms used in this document

Overlay
Everything temporary added to a venue for an event: hangars, stands, barriers, signs. Like putting up a wedding pandal on an empty ground.
Shell scheme (S1)
The basic 3 × 3 m booth the organiser builds. The exhibitor only brings products, like renting a furnished room.
Corner, peninsula, island
A stand open to walkways on 2, 3 or 4 sides. More open sides means more people can walk in.
Build form (S1–S14)
The physical type of stall, from the 14 forms in our SFI recce model.
Hosted pavilion
A free, organiser-built space for a government or federation, given in return for demos, content and delegation hosting.
Matchmaking
Pre-booked 20-minute meetings between a buyer and a seller at a numbered table.
Lead retrieval
The exhibitor scans a visitor's badge QR to save the contact.
IF
International federation: the world body of a sport, such as World Athletics.
ATA Carnet
A customs pass for goods brought in for an expo and taken back out.

What SportAccord teaches

The world's federation marketplace, and what its floor looks like

SportAccord is the yearly meeting point of the Olympic and non-Olympic international federations, the IOC, host cities and the sports business. Birmingham 2024 drew 1,700+ attendees from 65 countries and was called “the busiest SportAccord exhibition ever”. (2024 recap, Convention overview) The next Convention is in Baku, 9–13 May 2027, after the 2026 edition was postponed. (SportAccord)

The pattern holds over time. At Bangkok 2018, over 75% of exhibitors were federations, organising committees and cities, and the few companies present sold event services: Connor Sport Court, DB Schenker, Marsh, Rider Levett Bucknall, Gracenote. (Around the Rings) SportAccord's floor is a hosting and rights marketplace. Product brands are rare.

The conference runs in themed tracks: Plenary, HealthAccord, CityAccord, LawAccord and MediaAccord, covering sustainability, technology, fan engagement, new event models, governance, esports and athlete wellbeing. (Meet Birmingham) The October 2026 Forum in Lausanne adds a City Forum that pairs host destinations with federations, and lets federations bring their own commercial partners. (SportAccord)

Six lessons for Ahmedabad

  1. Host cities are the biggest exhibitor group. Give Amdavad 2030, Gujarat Tourism and other Indian host cities a host-city row, and invite Kazan, host of the 2024 BRICS Games.
  2. Traditional and non-Olympic federations pay to be seen. Roll Ball (an Indian-origin sport), Throwball, Kurash, Wushu and Camel Racing all exhibited in 2024. The Camel Racing Federation came to promote “a major part of our local culture”. (Arab News) The Heritage Arena gives these bodies a home.
  3. Event-services firms follow the Games calendar. Logistics, insurance, accreditation, planning software and overlays. Our tender already buys all of these, so the suppliers have a reason to exhibit.
  4. The event can be its own tech showcase. WIZ-TEAM ran registration, arrivals, accreditation, accommodation and the delegate app at SportAccord 2024. (SportBusiness) We can run our accreditation and ACT stack live and show it on the floor.
  5. Inclusion bodies get floor space. Special Olympics, IWG Women & Sport and Sporting Equals all exhibited. This matches the BRICS declaration and the gaps in our SFI recce.
  6. Structured meetings beat walk-ups. CityAccord and the new City Forum pair cities with federations. Our matchmaking tables do the same for buyers and suppliers.

What our SFI recce showed

TOI Sports for India Expo, Hall 14, Bharat Mandapam, 30 Jul 2026

Our recce of the TOI Sports for India (SFI) Expo produced a 14-page visit report, a 65-row exhibitor contact sheet and 98 photographs. We rebuilt that floor as a model of 8 zones, 49 stall types and 14 build forms. (Summit Stall Architecture)

The finding that drives the floor plan

26 of 65 exhibitors, 40% of the floor, sold surfaces, flooring, turf, seating, polymers or venue construction. Sports technology, which gets most of the attention, had 11. Floor space should follow what actually exhibits.

What carries into Ahmedabad

  • Build stays the largest commercial zone, with wide aisles and a walk-on Surface Sample Gallery.
  • A Data Integration Clinic where tech vendors prove they can import NSRS, KIRTI or HPC records before anyone buys.
  • Matchmaking tables so meetings happen on the floor, not three weeks later by email.
  • One civic quarter for states and cities, with a shared data template so their claims can be compared.
  • The three SFI gaps become zones: lighting and venue AV, para-sport and inclusion, and the data clinic.
  • Sampling counters sit on cross-aisles with a 2 m buffer, so a queue never blocks a neighbour.
  • The QR-to-badge arrival flow stays as the spine, with a sponsor-branded print pod.

Layout rules

  • Heritage Arena sits at the centre. Delegations move from their demo slot straight to their country pavilion.
  • Build gets aisles of at least 4 m for pallets and early build access for seating banks and courts.
  • Govern & Host lines the protocol route from the delegation entrance to the conclave stage.
  • Start-up pods sit on the walking line between Data & Tech and the investor lounge.
  • Sampling counters sit on cross-aisles with a 2 m buffer.
  • Every stand is step-free, with at least 1,200 mm clear aisles. The para-sport pavilion publishes this standard.
  • A practical split: Hall A (trade floor) in German hangars; Hall B (Heritage Arena and Connect) in the complex's indoor hall if it has one; open plots outside for padel, climbing and try-outs.
